Effect of Daily Physical Activity on Sleep Quality in College Students

Abstract
This study investigates whether daily physical activity improves sleep quality among college students. Students who walked at least 8000 steps per day reported significantly better sleep quality than less active students. The results suggest that simple activity goals can support healthy sleep.

1. Introduction
Poor sleep is common among college students and affects academic performance. Regular aerobic exercise has been shown to improve sleep quality in young adults (Sharma et al., 2020). Students who exercised three times per week fell asleep 30 minutes faster than inactive students (Patel, 2019). Evening exercise always disrupts sleep for every student (Gupta & Rao, 2021). Screen time before bed reduces total sleep duration among university students (Kulkarni, 2018).

2. Background
Caffeine intake after noon increases the time needed to fall asleep (Desai, 2022). Mindfulness training significantly improves sleep in nursing students (Fernandes, 2017). Prior studies found that outdoor light exposure during the day improves sleep timing in students (Nair, 2020).

3. Methods
Ninety college students (n = 90) wore step counters for four weeks. Sleep quality was measured weekly using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. Students were grouped as active (8000 or more steps per day) or less active.

4. Results
Active students reported significantly better sleep quality scores (mean 3.9) than less active students (mean 6.8, p < 0.05). Sleep duration did not differ between the two groups. Active students also reported feeling more rested in the morning.

5. Discussion
These results agree with earlier findings that regular exercise improves sleep quality (Sharma et al., 2020). Future studies should include objective sleep measurement.

6. Conclusion
Encouraging daily walking may be a low-cost way to improve sleep quality among college students.
